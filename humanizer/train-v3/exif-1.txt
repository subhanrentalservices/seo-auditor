=== original (live page)
Everything about EXIF follows from one fact: the data is part of the photo file. It isn't kept in your phone's settings or in the cloud. It goes wherever the file goes, whether that's an email attachment, a USB stick, a cloud folder or a file transfer. You don't see it in normal viewing, only when you open a photo's info panel or a metadata viewer.

Most cameras and phones write EXIF automatically into JPEG and HEIC photos. PNG and WebP files can carry metadata too, though screenshots and graphics usually carry much less.

EXIF is also only one kind of photo metadata. Editing apps and photo agencies use XMP and IPTC metadata, which can hold captions, keywords, copyright notices and editing history. "Photo metadata" or "image metadata" covers all of these, and EXIF is the part your camera writes.

=== A how-to site 2015
The important thing to understand about EXIF is that it lives inside the image file itself. Your phone doesn't keep a separate record of it somewhere in its settings, and it isn't stored on some server in the cloud. If you email a photo to a friend, copy it to a flash drive, or upload it to Dropbox, the EXIF data goes right along with it. You just won't notice, because image viewers don't show it unless you go looking in the file's properties or open it in a dedicated metadata tool.

Pretty much every digital camera and smartphone writes EXIF data to JPEG files automatically, and newer iPhones do the same with HEIC. PNG and WebP images can hold metadata as well, but screenshots and web graphics tend to have very little in them.

It's also worth pointing out that EXIF isn't the only kind of metadata a photo can have. Programs like Lightroom and Photoshop, along with stock photo agencies, use formats called XMP and IPTC to store things like captions, keywords, copyright info, and a history of edits. When people talk about "photo metadata" they usually mean all of it, while EXIF is just the part that comes from the camera.
